How many earthquakes of each size?

Every earthquake the Japan Meteorological Agency catalogued, counted by magnitude. Small quakes vastly outnumber large ones, roughly ten times more for each step down in magnitude (the Gutenberg–Richter law). Where the curve bends away from that straight line at the small end, the network was missing quakes, not the earth.

Reading this honestly

  • Mc, the magnitude of completeness, is the smallest magnitude above which essentially every quake in the selection was recorded. It's estimated from the data: the lowest magnitude at which a Gutenberg–Richter line explains at least 95% (or 90%) of the counts; failing that, the most common magnitude plus 0.2. Below Mc, counts say more about the instruments than about the earth.
  • b is the slope of the line above Mc. Well-covered regions over a short period give values near 1. Mixing well- and poorly-covered areas (such as all of Japan), or periods with different detection (such as 1919–2024), gives lower values, because some quakes above the overall Mc were still being missed.
  • Eras are split at visible steps in JMA's processing: the 1975–76 jump in recorded events, the October 1997 start of the unified catalogue (when low-frequency events first appear), and 2016, when automatic solutions start appearing in large numbers.
  • Counts are JMA-located natural earthquakes with a magnitude whose epicentre lies in 24–46°N, 122–148°E. Low-frequency, artificial and volcanic events are left out. Magnitudes are JMA's own; for the very largest quakes that is the moment magnitude (e.g. 2011 Tōhoku, M9.0).
