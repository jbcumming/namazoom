Reading this honestly

Counts cover the map area you're looking at and the years on the timeline.

Mc, the magnitude of completeness, is the smallest magnitude above which essentially every quake was recorded, estimated from the data (where a Gutenberg–Richter line fits 95% or 90% of the counts; failing that, the most common magnitude + 0.2). Below Mc, counts say more about the instruments than about the earth.

b is the slope above Mc. Well-covered areas over short periods give values near 1; mixing land and sea, or periods with different detection, gives lower values.

Eras split at visible steps in JMA's processing: the 1975–76 jump in recorded events, the October 1997 unified catalogue, and 2016, when automatic solutions start appearing in large numbers.

JMA-located natural earthquakes with a magnitude, epicentre in 24–46°N, 122–148°E. Magnitudes are JMA's own; for the largest quakes that is the moment magnitude (e.g. 2011 Tōhoku, M9.0).
