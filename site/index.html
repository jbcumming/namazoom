About Namazoom

A century of Japan's earthquakes on a map, from the Japan Meteorological Agency's records since 1919.

Namazu 鯰

In Japanese folklore, earthquakes come from the namazu, a giant catfish that lives in the mud beneath Japan. The god of Kashima Shrine pins it down with a great stone, the kaname-ishi (要石), but when he looks away the catfish thrashes and the ground shakes. After the great Edo earthquake of 1855, cheap catfish prints (namazu-e, 鯰絵) filled the city.

The namazu is still Japan's earthquake mascot, on early-warning logos and road signs. The Namazoom logo is a homage to it.
